Why we asked

The gap between a living tradition and a museum piece

Some background on the pattern this survey was built to test.

Digital and economic change has hit rural communities across Europe hard, and one of the clearest signs is the slow erosion of living identity paired with a steady outflow of young people. Traditional crafts, oral folklore and local customs are increasingly treated by young people as relics for a museum wall rather than something they could actually use to build a life at home.

That cultural distance is made worse by a real lack of local opportunity, which pushes young people toward big cities and quietly wears down the ties between generations that used to hold rural communities together. UNESCO has pointed out that connecting young people to intangible cultural heritage through hands-on, non-formal learning is one of the more effective ways to protect local identity and support sustainable community development. Eurostat's demographic data backs this up from a different angle: the working-age and youth population in the EU's rural regions is shrinking by roughly 0.6 to 0.7 percent a year, with more than 160 regions facing acute depopulation.

To see how that plays out in its own cross-border network, rather than assume, Asociația Vechea Bucovină asked young people directly. The findings below are what shaped "Rural Heritage Without Borders" from the ground up.

How this data was collected

A quick note on who ran it, who answered, and when.

Who ran it, and when

The survey ran on the Rural Heritage Data Hub tool from May through August 2026, led by Asociația Vechea Bucovină (Romania) during the preparation stage of the "Rural Heritage Without Borders" youth exchange. Respondents were young people connected to rural or small-town communities, reached through the six partner organizations' local networks.
